Assessment practices

Grade 11 English Home Language · Term 3 revision quiz: poetry, film literacy, philosophy enrichment and The Tempest, Acts 1 and 2

Grade and subject
Grade 11, English Home Language
Curriculum context
CAPS English Home Language, FET Phase, Term 3: Literature (poetry and drama), Language in context (visual literacy and film), and the enrichment unit. Informal assessment (assessment for learning) consolidating the term's work before the formal Term 3 assessments.
Type of task
Team revision quiz, 26 multiple-choice questions in six rounds, run on Kahoot. I wrote every question and the memorandum.
Marks and time
36 marks (one mark per question; bonus and gauntlet questions count double) · about 35 minutes
Taught
Abbotts College Pretoria East, September 2026, five Grade 11 classes

Learner instructions

  1. You will work in teams of three or four. Choose a team name and one device per team. Join the Kahoot game with the PIN on the board.
  2. There are six rounds: three poems ("For Oom Piet", "The Herb Garden", "The Poet"), film literacy, philosophy and the screen, and The Tempest Acts 1 and 2.
  3. Each question is multiple choice with one correct answer. Read the whole question before you answer. You have 30 seconds per question.
  4. Bonus questions at the end of a round count double. Every question in the final Tempest round, the Gauntlet, counts double.
  5. Discuss each answer in your team before you tap. Talk, but do not shout the answer across the room.
  6. No notes and no textbook. This is revision: it shows you and me what you already know and what we still need to work on.
  7. The winning team earns a Sleep Token: the right to rest your head on the desk for one future lesson, no questions asked.

Assessment instrument

The correct answers are given in the memorandum below. In the quiz the options appear in random order.

Round 1: "For Oom Piet" (Finuala Dowling)

  1. Stanza 1: the toddler leading the mother across the road is an example of: A) antithesis B) hyperbole C) role inversion D) personification
  2. Oom Piet uses tones for "bilious dogs". What does "bilious" mean here? A) aggressive or rabid B) sickly or nauseous C) overly affectionate D) loud and barking
  3. The line "You guessed" implies what about Oom Piet? A) he incorrectly assumed why she was crying B) he was playing a game to entertain the toddler C) he guessed her daughter's name incorrectly D) he intuitively understood her pain without prying
  4. The repetition of hissing sounds in "the sense of a rescue" is called: A) assonance B) consonance C) alliteration D) sibilance
  5. Bonus (double): what is the concept of theoxeny? A) the sacred, unbreakable Greek law of guest-host hospitality B) a humble stranger reveals themselves as a disguised deity C) a god suddenly appears to resolve a hopeless situation D) the act of making offerings to household ancestors

Round 2: "The Herb Garden" (Stephen Gray)

  1. "Before I was ready": literally, unready to garden. Metaphorically: A) unprepared for the physical labour of maintaining a home B) emotionally unready for her death and imposed legacy C) unforgiving of his mother for rigid English traditions D) unwilling to accept his own ageing
  2. The "heart-stopping drought" is primarily a metaphor for: A) the devastating effects of climate change in South Africa B) the emotional barrenness and numbness of grief C) the speaker's literal lack of tears at the funeral D) the physical withering of the herb garden after her death
  3. The sensory imagery of mint and sage on fingers and nails demonstrates: A) the physical exhaustion of maintaining a dying garden B) her memory has become an inseparable, physical experience C) the harshness of the South African soil resisting plants D) he is trying to wash away the physical memory of grief
  4. What is the structural significance of the single full stop at the end? A) it creates a caesura that forces the reader to pause B) it signals a shift from an elegiac to a narrative tone C) it represents the absolute finality of physical death D) it indicates the speaker has finally forgotten his mother
  5. Bonus (double): what defines an elegy? A) a speech delivered at a funeral praising the deceased B) an impassioned, unstructured outpouring of raw grief C) a structured poem, typically a lament for the dead D) a musical composition or mass for the dead

Round 3: "The Poet" (Shakespeare)

  1. This excerpt about the poet, the lover and the lunatic is from which play? A) Macbeth B) The Tempest C) A Midsummer Night's Dream D) Romeo and Juliet
  2. Theseus describes the poet's eye rolling in a "fine frenzy". This is a: A) paradox B) hyperbole C) oxymoron D) metonymy
  3. The original Greek word "poiesis" translates directly to: A) dreaming B) speaking C) feeling D) making
  4. "Gives to airy nothing a local habitation" relies on what device? A) sibilance B) antithesis C) paradox D) hyperbole
  5. Bonus (double): what is the deep irony of Duke Theseus delivering this speech? A) he uses fairy magic to deliver his ruling on rationality B) he claims to be the only sane person while hallucinating C) a strict rationalist uses soaring poetry to mock poetry D) he praises the poet's imagination while executing lovers

Round 4: Film literacy and visual coding

  1. Filmmakers construct meaning; viewers interpret it through personal context: A) denotation and connotation B) index and icon C) encoding and decoding D) mise-en-scène and setting
  2. Which lighting simulates a character losing reality to a dream state? A) low-key lighting B) back lighting C) overexposure D) high-key lighting
  3. Which role uses leitmotifs for emotional depth and character continuity? A) the editor B) the director C) the composer D) the producer
  4. Which technique establishes dominance and vulnerability without words? A) overexposure B) high and low camera angles C) extreme close-ups D) leitmotifs

Round 5: Philosophy and the screen

  1. Proving that the speaker in a poem exists tests how you verify knowledge: A) axiology B) epistemology C) metaphysics D) logic
  2. Analysing the fundamental rules of reality in films such as The Matrix uses: A) epistemology B) axiology C) metaphysics D) logic
  3. Characters debating reality (Paprika) versus breaking the fourth wall (Deadpool): A) meta / literal B) literal / meta C) symbolic / literal D) literal / symbolic
  4. Analysing how a character reasons versus what they fundamentally value is: A) epistemology / metaphysics B) logic / axiology C) utility / deontology D) axiology / logic
  5. Bonus (double): "I avoid AI because it makes me stagnant, not flourishing." Which ethical frame? A) utility B) relational C) deontology D) virtue

Round 6: The Tempest, Acts 1 and 2: the Gauntlet (all double)

  1. Why did Sycorax imprison Ariel in a pine tree for twelve years? A) Ariel plotted with Caliban to steal her magical texts B) Ariel was too delicate for her abhorred commands C) Ariel tried to warn ships away, ruining her spells D) Ariel refused to acknowledge her authority over the isle
  2. What is Antonio's logic to convince Sebastian to assassinate Alonso? A) the island's magic will erase Gonzalo's memory B) Alonso's men are planning mutiny, so Sebastian will be a hero C) Ferdinand is dead and Claribel is too far away to claim the throne D) Prospero secretly promised to legitimise Sebastian's rule
  3. When Trinculo finds Caliban, what is his immediate capitalistic thought? A) he believes Caliban is a deity and bargains for passage B) he considers taking him to England to exhibit for money C) he assumes Caliban is a wealthy merchant he can rob D) he thinks Caliban is a demon sent to punish his sins
  4. Why does Prospero immediately treat Ferdinand harshly and enslave him? A) he genuinely fears Ferdinand is a spy plotting with Antonio B) he is testing Ariel's loyalty C) he is overcome with rage at seeing his enemy's son D) he wants the courtship to be hard so that Ferdinand values Miranda highly

One mark per correct answer; bonus questions and all Gauntlet questions count two. Kahoot records each team's answers and the leaderboard, which I keep as evidence of the class's results.

Feedback and differentiation

Feedback. The feedback is immediate and whole-class: after every question Kahoot shows the correct answer and how many teams chose each option, and I stop to explain any question that more than a third of the room got wrong, going back to the line of the poem or the scene of the play. The round scores tell me which text or skill needs re-teaching before the formal assessment, and I open the next lesson with that. Teams talk through every answer before they commit, so learners also give each other feedback as they go.

Differentiation. Teams are mixed, so that stronger readers explain their reasoning to weaker ones and every learner takes part without being exposed. Questions are graded within each round from recall (which play, which device) to interpretation (irony, structure), and the double-point bonus questions stretch the strongest learners without punishing the rest. Thirty seconds per question is enough for discussion but short enough to keep attention. The quiz needs no reading of long passages, which helps learners who read slowly, and the Sleep Token prize keeps the stakes playful. For a learner who cannot take part on a device, the same quiz runs on paper, as described on the Technology page.
